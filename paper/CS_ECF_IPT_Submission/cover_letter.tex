\begin{letter}{Editors\\Infrared Physics \& Technology}
\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please consider our manuscript, ``Confidence-Shielded
Evaluator-Conditioned Fusion for Risk-Controlled Infrared--Visible Object
Detection,'' for publication in \emph{Infrared Physics \& Technology}.

Infrared--visible fusion can make thermal evidence available to object
detectors, but the same fused image does not benefit every detector. We show
that fusion utility changes across detector checkpoints and formulate selection
as a label-free input-arbitration problem. The proposed Confidence-Shielded
Evaluator-Conditioned Fusion (CS-ECF) evaluates visible, CDF, PIAFusion, and
SPGFusion candidates with the target detector. A fused input is used only when
its top-five confidence exceeds that of the visible image by a factor of 1.5;
otherwise, the system preserves the visible input as a safety fallback.

Across LLVIP, M3FD, and an independent official-test subset of DroneVehicle,
CS-ECF improved 9 of 12 detector checkpoints. The dataset-balanced AP50 gain was
0.0133, with a detector-by-group hierarchical 95\% interval of [0.0028, 0.0213].
Removing the shield increased the worst observed AP50 loss from 0.0040 to
0.0584. The two DroneVehicle checkpoints improved by 0.0251 and 0.0293; they
belong to the same training run and are reported as correlated checks rather
than independent architectures. A thermal-mask ablation further showed a small
but consistent advantage over global detail injection.

We believe the work is relevant to the journal's readership because it addresses
how thermal information should be admitted into a deployed detection system,
uses real infrared--visible datasets, and reports both benefit and cost. The
analysis also identifies an important boundary: M3FD gains are concentrated in
the people class, and the LLVIP intervals cross zero. We therefore present
CS-ECF as a risk-controlled evaluator-conditioned system, not as evidence of
uniform improvement over all object classes.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under
consideration by another journal. All authors have approved the submission and
declare no competing interests. This research did not receive any specific grant
from funding agencies in the public, commercial, or not-for-profit sectors. Data
and code availability are stated in the manuscript.
