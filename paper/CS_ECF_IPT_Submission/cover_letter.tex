\begin{letter}{Editors\\Infrared Physics \& Technology}
\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please consider our manuscript, ``Confidence-Shielded
Evaluator-Conditioned Fusion for Risk-Controlled Infrared--Visible Object
Detection,'' for publication in \emph{Infrared Physics \& Technology}.

Infrared--visible fusion can make thermal evidence available to object
detectors, but the same fused image does not benefit every detector. We
formulate input selection as a label-free arbitration problem. The proposed
Confidence-Shielded Evaluator-Conditioned Fusion (CS-ECF) evaluates visible,
consensus-detail fusion (CDF), PIAFusion, and SPGFusion candidates with the
target detector. A fused input is used only when its top-five confidence reaches
1.5 times that of visible; otherwise, visible is retained as a safety fallback.

Across LLVIP, M3FD, and an official-test subset of DroneVehicle, nine of 12
checkpoint point estimates improved. No individual checkpoint contrast
survived Holm correction and the sign test was not significant (\(p=0.073\)).
The prespecified dataset-balanced AP50 gain was 0.0133, with a
training-run-by-group hierarchical 95\% interval of [0.0026, 0.0261].
Removing the shield increased the worst observed AP50 loss from 0.0040 to
0.0584.

Against the stronger Always CDF baseline, the dataset-balanced difference was
0.0102 AP50, with a training-run-by-group interval of [-0.00057, 0.02980].
The interval included zero; the point estimate was concentrated in M3FD and
DroneVehicle and was not present on LLVIP. We therefore describe a
conditional risk-control contribution, not dominance over the best fixed fusion
input.

Three additional evaluators were not used in policy construction. Two
COCO-pretrained models improved AP50 over visible by 0.0213 and 0.0250, with
scene-group intervals above zero, but neither separated from Always CDF. An
LLVIP-fine-tuned YOLOv9t then provided a negative boundary: CS-ECF lost 0.0108
AP50 relative to visible, although the shield improved the unshielded selector
by 0.0153. Its calibration error was about five times larger than those of the
zero-shot evaluators, and 41\% of its switched images degraded. We report this
counterexample because confidence arbitration is conditional rather than
universally portable.

We believe the work is relevant to the journal's readership because it addresses
how thermal information should be admitted into a deployed detection system and
reports its benefit, cost, and failure modes on real infrared--visible data.
M3FD gains are concentrated in the people class, LLVIP intervals cross zero,
and the fine-tuned evaluator reverses the visible-relative sign. We therefore
present CS-ECF as a bounded evaluator-conditioned system, not as evidence of
uniform improvement or universal transfer.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under
consideration by another journal. An earlier, related manuscript by the same
authors on evaluator-dependent fidelity-index rankings was previously submitted
to this journal and was not accepted. It is not under consideration elsewhere.
The present manuscript is a distinct method study; it does not reproduce the
earlier fidelity-ranking claims, and all shared datasets and evaluator
infrastructure are identified in the manuscript. All authors have approved the
submission and declare no competing interests. This research did not receive any
specific grant from funding agencies in the public, commercial, or
not-for-profit sectors. The manuscript includes an AI-use declaration and
statements for data, code, CRediT contributions, and competing interests.

Thank you for your consideration.
