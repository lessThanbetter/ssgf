Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider our manuscript, ``Fidelity Metrics Do Not Reliably Rank
Visible--Infrared Fusion Rules for Pedestrian Detection,'' for publication as an
original research article in \emph{The Visual Computer}.

Image-fidelity scores are routinely used to rank visible-infrared fusion rules
before a downstream visual detector is trained. Our study asks whether that ranking
remains valid when the fused images are fixed and the detector and metric
implementation are varied. We evaluate fourteen fused inputs with 26 detection
checkpoints on LLVIP and an aligned FLIR-ADAS benchmark, and add a secondary
segmentation comparison on MSRS.

With all 300 LLVIP test images held out, all 42 comparisons under three
visible-trained pedestrian detectors are negative at mAP50, with deficits from
0.046 to 0.246. A detector trained on both modalities reverses the sign for four
inputs by 0.022 to 0.029 in every run. Two constructions of the same VIF family rank
SPGFusion first and last. Paired image intervals, four independent training runs,
two source conventions and alignment-stratified analyses show that the discrepancy
is not explained by ordinary sampling noise. The evidence leads to a practical rule:
fidelity should not be the sole criterion for selecting a fusion rule.

We believe the work fits \emph{The Visual Computer} because it examines the
reliability of visual-quality metrics at the intersection of image processing,
multimodal perception and computer vision. It provides a reproducible evaluation
protocol and releases per-image scores, fused images, split manifests and analysis
scripts. The conclusions are bounded to the measured pedestrian-detection
populations, and the paper explicitly reports alignment and benchmark limitations.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has been
approved by all authors. The authors declare no competing interests. Data and code
availability are stated in the manuscript.
